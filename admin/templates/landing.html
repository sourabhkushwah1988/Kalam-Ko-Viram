A Living Tradition

The Journey of Urdu Poetry

17th Century
Wali Deccani and the Deccan School

Often called the father of Urdu ghazal, Wali brought the language of the Deccan into a refined poetic form that later travelled north.

18th Century
Mir Taqi Mir and Delhi's Golden Age

Mir's melancholic simplicity set the emotional register for the ghazal that generations of poets would inherit.

19th Century
Ghalib and the Philosophical Turn

Ghalib pushed the ghazal toward abstraction and philosophy, layering wit with existential doubt.

Early 20th Century
Iqbal and the Poetry of Awakening

Iqbal's verse turned toward nationhood, self-realisation and spiritual reform, reaching far beyond literary circles.

Mid-to-Late 20th Century
The Progressive and Modern Voices

Faiz, Faraz, Jaun Elia and their contemporaries carried the ghazal and nazm into the language of protest, exile and modern longing.

Today
A Digital Diwan

Platforms like Kalam Ko Viram carry this centuries-old tradition into a new generation of readers and writers.
